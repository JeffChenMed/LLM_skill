The second prerequisite is that a great paper must tell a story. The research needs to be communicated in a clear, concise and coherent way, so that readers understand what you did, what you found, and how you reached your conclusions – otherwise, your research is falling at the last hurdle.

## Writing Principles

- A strong scientific paper should tell a coherent, clear story, guiding readers through what was done, what was found, and how conclusions were reached.
- Editors (specifically at Nature) prioritize papers that will interest a broad cross-disciplinary scientific audience.
- Two main criteria for high-impact are:
  1. Broad interest: Results or ideas that are novel and/or have clear relevance outside the immediate field.
  2. Conceptual advance: Work that significantly changes or advances understanding of a field.
- Mechanistic insight and the potential to inspire further research are also valued, as illustrated by specific examples cited.
