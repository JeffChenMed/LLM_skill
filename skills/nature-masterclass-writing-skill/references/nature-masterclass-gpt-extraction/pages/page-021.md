**Abstracts**

Different fields and different journals have different requirements and conventions when it comes to abstracts. However, generally speaking, your abstract should be a mini-version of your paper – it needs to reflect what is in the manuscript so that readers know what your work is and whether it’s worth reading about.

## Writing Principles

1. **Be declarative in scientific writing:** Clearly state your findings or effects rather than just mentioning their existence.
2. **Engage your audience:** Write specifically for your intended readership, but maintain accuracy and avoid exaggeration.
3. **Highlight only the main novel finding:** Keep the focus in titles and summaries on the key new result, rather than summarizing everything.
4. **Choose keywords carefully:** Ensure that your title includes critical keywords relevant to your work’s main concepts and context.
5. **Avoid questions in titles:** Formulate titles as statements to be more assertive and informative.
6. **Avoid puns and wordplay:** They may confuse or distract, especially for non-native English speakers, and are generally less effective than clear language.
7. **Abstracts should reflect the paper:** Write the abstract as a condensed version of your manuscript to inform potential readers of the core work and value.
