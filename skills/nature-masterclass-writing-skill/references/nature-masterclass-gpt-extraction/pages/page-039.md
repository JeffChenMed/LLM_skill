**Journal Decisions and Appeals**

**Decisions**

After the paper has been reviewed by the referees, the editors will make a decision. This decision will be based on the reviewers’ advice, but editors at Nature Research journals do not just ‘count votes’ — referee reports can be contradictory, as referees will have diverse technical expertise and opinions. Instead, the editor will make an informed decision based on all the information available. The editor makes his or her own decision, based on the referees’ comments and their own experience, expertise and judgement. This sometimes involves overruling one or more of the referees. 

There are several possible decisions:
- **Accept**, with or without editorial revisions
- Invite the authors to **revise** their manuscript to address specific concerns before a final decision is reached
- **Reject**, but indicate to the authors that further work might justify a **resubmission**
- **Reject outright**, typically on grounds of specialist interest, lack of novelty, insufficient conceptual advance or major technical and/or interpretational problems

If you are advised to revise and invited to resubmit, only do so when you are able to comprehensively address all comments. You need to remain professional and remember that the referees are only human and can make mistakes.

## Writing Principles

1. **Politeness and Constructiveness**: Strive to be polite and constructive in your feedback, aiming to help the authors improve their work.
2. **Thoroughness**: Address every comment or concern raised by reviewers if you plan to resubmit.
3. **Decision Types**: Understand there are several editorial decisions after peer review—acceptance, invitation to revise, rejection with resubmission potential, or outright rejection.
4. **Informative Decisions**: Editorial decisions are informed by but not strictly bound to peer reviewer votes; editors use judgement and experience.
5. **Professionalism**: Maintain professionalism and acknowledge that referees (reviewers) are human and prone to occasional error.
