## Transcription

Don't forget that editors are under no obligation to read the letter! If it’s more than a few pages long, they might skip through it – try to stick to somewhere around one page of text (not crammed together; excluding contact details/referee suggestions).

This structure works well in most cases:

---
Dear Editor,

We would like to submit our manuscript entitled [title] as a [format] in [journal name].

To our knowledge, this is the first report showing... This is important because...

We believe our findings would appeal to the readership of [journal name]...

Possible referees we would suggest...
We look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience...
---

The basics:
- Title of the paper
- Format (e.g. Article/Letter)

The pitch:
- Very brief context of direct relevance to your findings
- Key finding (related to context)
- Why is it important?
- Interest to that journal’s readers

Other requirements:
- Referees for in/exclusion
- Authors and affiliations
- Contact information

For many journals, some of these requirements can be conveyed separately in the submission system.

Note that almost all journals allow you to exclude or recommend referees. Editors will generally carefully research these suggestions. They are not obliged to include or exclude them, and a good editor will never pick more than one inclusion suggestion, but the exclusions are almost always honoured within reason.

## Writing Principles

- Keep your cover letter concise: aim for one page, excluding contact information or referee suggestions.
- Follow a clear structure: open with the manuscript title, format, and journal name.
- Immediately establish relevance: briefly explain the context, key findings, and importance of your work.
- Tailor your pitch: make clear why your work is of interest to the journal's readers.
- Provide suggestions for referees and clearly state any exclusions if allowed.
- Include your authorship and affiliations, as well as necessary contact information.
- Understand journal submission systems may split these elements; be adaptable.
- Remember exclusion of suggested referees is more frequently honored than inclusion.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Use a direct, professional salutation ("Dear Editor").
- Structure the letter logically: introduction, context, significance, journal fit, and practical matters (e.g., referees, contact details).
- Use bullet points or clear paragraph breaks for requirements and explanations.
- Avoid excessive length and dense text blocks; editors appreciate clarity and brevity.
- Make the "pitch" prominent—editors need to know quickly why your manuscript matters.
