3. **“Here we show…”**

This is found in a lot of papers in Nature Research journals. It’s the headline conclusion of the study, and should be just one sentence. It’s placed in the middle to contrast directly with the gap you just presented, and to focus the reader before they read the more detailed findings that led to this conclusion. 4. **The overall approach, key results and conclusions**

Details of the approach should be short, limited to the most important details of the experimental design, and preferably incorporated into the description of the results, e.g. “Using… we…”. You should include a few key bits of data if possible, but don’t overload with numbers; focus on the most important findings that underpin the main conclusions. You need to include enough information to be useful to readers but not so much that you choke the abstract and detract from the main message. The main paper provides the detail.

5. **The advance over previous work, the implications**

How does this change our understanding of the field? What are the immediate implications? A couple of sentences here will help readers understand where your work leads. That said, do not hype the implications – instead, be realistic and focus on the immediate implications. Please note that some fields and journals do not include this part of the abstract.

## Writing Principles

- When describing the research problem or gap, specify knowns and unknowns, and explain why the uncertainty matters; aim to state your research objective clearly.
- Use a brief, focused statement (e.g., “Here we show…”) as a headline conclusion early on, especially in journals that expect this structure.
- Summarize your approach, emphasizing only the most essential experimental details, and make sure to integrate key results and conclusions succinctly. Overloading with technical detail or data can obscure the main message.
- Describe how your work advances the field and what the immediate implications are, but remain realistic and avoid overstating the significance. Note that not all disciplines or journals expect this.

## Style / Structure Notes

- There is a logical sequence: describe the problem/gap, state the main new finding, summarize key methods & results, then frame the advance or implications.
- Use concise sentences, avoid excessive detail in the abstract, and gear the presentation/order towards clarity and reader understanding.
- The “Here we show…” sentence serves as a turning point, connecting the presented problem directly to the major result.
- Balance informativeness with brevity—enough detail for utility, not so much as to overwhelm.
