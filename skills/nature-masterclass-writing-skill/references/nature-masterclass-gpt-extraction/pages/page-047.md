## Writing Principles

- Present statistical data (such as averages) from multiple independent experiments to give a more robust and informative analysis, rather than relying solely on measures like standard deviation.
- Avoid misleading normalization in figures; normalization should accurately reflect the experimental conditions and controls.
- Draw conclusions in the text that are strongly supported by the data; if the effect is not significant, modify the conclusions accordingly.
- Pay attention to the clarity and correct order of figure legends/captions for reader comprehension.
- Provide commentary or discussion in the text when results differ between experiments, conditions, or cell lines, especially if those differences appear in supplementary data.
- Consider potential confounding issues with experimental design or data, such as low counts in a particular cell phase, and address how these issues may affect the study’s conclusions.
