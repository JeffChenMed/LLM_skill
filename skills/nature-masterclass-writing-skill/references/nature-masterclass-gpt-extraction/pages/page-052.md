## Writing Principles

- Ensure your manuscript is coherent and easy to read, presenting information as a logical story.
- State the manuscript’s thesis clearly at the outset; make sure Results and Discussion sections address key points and draw clear conclusions.
- Construct paragraphs with logical flow and clear transitions. Begin with broad concepts and develop toward specifics. Limit each paragraph to a single idea or theme.
- Write sentences that are well-structured and interconnected, favoring active voice for clarity.
- Maintain conciseness throughout; remove redundancy and avoid overlap between sections.
- Reference all necessary information to back up claims or provide context.
- Use consistent terminology, units, and scales for clarity and precision.
- Create a title that is concise, informative about the main findings, and contains essential keywords for effective indexing.
- Structure the abstract to stand alone as a summary. Clearly state objectives, scope, key results, principal conclusions, and the broader context of the findings.
