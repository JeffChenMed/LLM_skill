You should have a clear rationale for each decision. As you do not have the full paper, you will have to make the decision based on the abstract only (Nature Research Editors always have access to the full article, unless it is a pre-submission inquiry).

The objective of this exercise is to apply what you have learned about what makes a great manuscript, and to stimulate discussion. We will take a vote at the end of this exercise to see which summary you would send to peer review if you were an Editor. Your group does not need to agree!

First, try to work out what the authors have achieved in each case. Then, look for the following:
- **Broad impact:** Are researchers in other fields likely to find the novel conclusions of scientific interest, or to be directly affected by them?
- **Significant impact:** Is there evidence that the novel conclusions could change the way of thinking among scientists in related fields?

Also be on the lookout for a clear, concise and coherent story. This should include a statement of the ‘gap’ to be explored, an objective/hypothesis, and specific, useful information about the findings.

## Writing Principles

- Provide a clear rationale for editorial decisions, even when information is limited to abstract-level.
- Assess abstracts for evidence of broad and significant scientific impact:
  - Broad impact involves relevance and potential interest to researchers outside the immediate field.
  - Significant impact means the conclusions could shift prevailing perspectives in related disciplines.
- Evaluate for overall clarity and coherence in the abstract:
  - Look for a clearly articulated knowledge gap or why the research matters.
  - Ensure the objective or hypothesis is expressed.
  - Confirm that the findings are concrete, specific, and useful.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Decision-making should be both reasoned and explicitly articulated.
- Abstracts are to be evaluated not merely on the presence of results, but on their relevance and potential influence beyond the immediate field.
- Summaries/stories within abstracts should be coherent and concise, guiding the reader logically from gap to hypothesis to findings.
- Use bullet points to highlight separate evaluative criteria for impact.
