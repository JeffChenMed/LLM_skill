The Methods Section

This section varies considerably from paper to paper, so is not covered in depth in this course. However, the golden rule is worth stating: you need to provide enough information for others to understand exactly what you did (and where the decision is important, why), and to repeat the experiments.

This typically means that you need to include information along the lines of:
- Your sample size, justification of that sample size and information about your samples/individuals
- The statistical tests you used (and whether or not the data meet the assumptions of those tests) – see the section on figures and tables

## Writing Principles

- The introduction should clearly state the gap, problem, or question being addressed, ideally with precise and succinct language. General context is not always necessary unless it directly supports the urgency or significance of the work.
- Avoid overloading introductions with unnecessary background if it detracts from the main objective/hypothesis.
- In methods sections, it is essential to provide enough detail for reproducibility; clarity and completeness are the highest priorities.
- Explicitly state sample sizes, with justification, and offer all necessary details about samples or individuals.
- Always report statistical tests used and clarify if data meet the required test assumptions.

- The text demonstrates how not all research articles have a conventional hypothesis, but must still justify their relevance.
