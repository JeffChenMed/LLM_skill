## Transcription

**The Discussion and Conclusion**

The Discussion section is where you evaluate your data and discuss the implications. Effectively, you need to answer two questions in the minds of critical readers:

1. *Why should I trust your data?*
2. *Why should I care?*

This is much easier said than done, which is why authors often complain that this section is hardest part of writing a paper. Take a look at the literature and you’ll find plenty of papers that make any number of these common mistakes:

- Basically repeating the Results section (or presenting the sort of basic interpretation that should have been in that section)
- Presenting new results
- Not addressing what you suspect are flaws in the argument
- Deviating from the main argument, e.g. dwelling on tedious nuances or engaging in rampant (and occasionally ridiculous) speculation

As with the other sections, you can view each part of the Discussion as having a specific function, and this can help you avoid falling into these traps.

## Writing Principles

- The Discussion section must **evaluate the data** and **interpret the implications**, not simply restate results.
- Address **two key questions** for the critical reader:  
  1. Why should the data be trusted (credibility, methodological soundness)?
  2. Why are the findings important (significance, broader implications)?
- Be aware of and avoid **common pitfalls**:
  - Repeating the Results section or offering only basic interpretation.
  - Introducing new, unreported results.
  - Failing to discuss potential weaknesses or flaws in the argument.
  - Losing focus by overanalyzing minor issues or speculating excessively.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Maintain a clear distinction between "Results" (what you found) and "Discussion" (what it means).
- The Discussion should **focus on interpretation and critical evaluation**.
- Address anticipated reader objections or questions directly.
- Keep the discussion **targeted and relevant**; avoid digressions and unfounded speculation.
- Structure the Discussion so that each part fulfills a clear purpose in supporting the overall thesis or message.
