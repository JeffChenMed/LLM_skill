## Transcription

Some key questions to ask yourself before writing:
- Why is the topic interesting?
- What is the broader context and impact of your work?
- Do your data compellingly support your conclusions?
- What’s been done before? Does your work change the current thinking in the field?
- Does your research deal with a big problem or issue in the field?

**Further work**

Take a look at a few papers in your field from Nature and compare them to papers in a leading specialist journal.

- Based on the criteria described here, do you see why the Nature editor wanted to publish the papers in that journal? Do you agree with each decision?
- If you think any of the papers in the more specialised journal might have been of editorial interest to Nature, what reasons could explain the fact that they weren’t published there?

## Writing Principles

- Before starting to write, critically evaluate your research by asking foundational questions about the topic, context, impact, support for conclusions, novelty, and significance.
- Comparing published papers in top-tier journals (like Nature) with those in specialist journals can clarify distinctions in editorial criteria and publication decisions.
- Understanding editorial priorities helps to frame your work’s relevance and potential appeal to broader audiences or high-impact outlets.
