There is nothing wrong with being uncertain about something, or offering a speculative explanation. No one expects you to know everything!

Here are some questions that can help you set your findings into context:
- Were there any surprises, or contradictions with previous work? If so, what does/might that tell us about this study or that previous work?
- Does it help resolve a controversy? Are there aspects still unresolved?
- Are there any features that couldn’t be explained? If so, what might that be telling us?

**Concluding your paper**

Ideally, the conclusion should offer something new to readers, and a particularly effective approach can be to look beyond your research – by outlining the implications of your findings on the field and related fields, and making suggestions for future research.

However, try to keep this short and focus on immediate implications. You should also consider the convention in your field – for example the implications in clinical research papers tend to be very limited or even absent, to avoid the risk of misleading clinicians.

## Writing Principles

- Always contextualize your findings by linking them back to established literature and the knowledge gap described in your Introduction.
- Explicitly explain how your findings advance the field beyond prior studies.
- Do not assume the reader will make complex connections themselves—articulate the significance and novelty of your work.
- It is acceptable to note uncertainties or offer speculative explanations; comprehensive knowledge isn’t expected.
- Use reflective questions to deepen your discussion:
  - Identify surprises or contradictions with previous studies.
  - Note whether your findings help resolve existing controversies or highlight remaining uncertainties.
  - Discuss features that remain unexplained and what they could imply.
- Conclude your paper by summarizing the broader implications of your findings and suggesting future research directions.
- Adapt the depth and scope of your conclusion to fit conventions in your field, especially regarding implications in clinical or sensitive research contexts.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Clearly signpost the transition into the conclusion (e.g., with a heading like “Concluding your paper”).
- Use bullet points to lay out practical reflective questions that help structure the discussion section.
- Encourage conciseness and relevance in the conclusion; focus on immediate implications unless field conventions suggest otherwise.
- Offer suggestions for future research in the conclusion, but avoid undue speculation, particularly in fields where it might be inappropriate or misleading.
