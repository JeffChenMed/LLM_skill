## Writing Principles

- **Redundant data/design features reduce clarity:** Avoid including any data or features in a figure that do not help clarify your message.
- **Every graph should have a clear title and legend:** Omit neither, as they guide interpretation.
- **Label axes properly:** Both axes must be precisely and unambiguously labeled.
- **Limit grid lines and framing:** Only include grid lines if essential for the reader to interpret the data. "Boxing in" a graph (top and right axes) is usually unnecessary.
- **Reduce visual clutter:** Excess tick marks and overly strong or numerous grid lines can overwhelm or distract.
- **Choose colours with care:** Make sure all lines/markers are visible and clear, even for those with colour vision deficiencies. Avoid using similar colours or those indistinguishable to colour blind readers (e.g., yellow on white).
- **Consider symbol distinctiveness:** Use symbols that are easily differentiable, especially for accessibility.
- **Appropriate axis scaling:** Begin axes at zero unless there is a compelling scientific reason not to, especially when data intervals are small, to avoid misleading the reader.
