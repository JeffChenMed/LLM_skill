## Transcription

We recommend using the active voice at least some of the time, rather than always using the passive voice. However, do check the requirements of your chosen journal – some journals still forbid the use of the active voice.

**Use tenses appropriately and consistently.** Although generally not problematic to native English-speakers, this can be challenging in certain situations, and for those more familiar with languages that lack tenses (such as oriental languages).

As a general rule, the present tense is used to describe:

- Current situations at time of writing (although if appropriate, consider providing data for a specific past year instead, using the past tense and citing a paper)
- Things that are generally known to be true ("protein kinases have a variety of functions") or that you propose to be generally true ("our findings suggest that there is an association between X and Y")
- The workings of equations and models ("in our model, X increases as Y increases"), and features of your figures etc. ("Figure 1 shows…"; "our data provide…"). Once published, these are essentially not ephemeral.
- Your narrative voice ("based on these findings, we propose that…")

Past tense is used to describe:

- Things that happened in the past! This includes what you did during a particular experiment, and what happened during those experiments. So you should not generally describe a method in the present tense "first do A, then do B" unless you are writing a protocol, as readers generally want to know what you did and what happened.
- We also suggest using the past tense for things like samples and people, e.g. "patient 1 had blue eyes", but this rule is less clear. Although in some cases you may make a statement that is still true at time of writing, samples still tend to be ephemeral – and unfortunately so are people!

## Writing Principles

- Use the **active voice** at least some of the time, but check journal guidelines as some still require passive voice.
- **Tenses must be used appropriately and consistently.**
    - Present tense is used for:
        - Describing current situations.
        - Stating general truths or widely accepted knowledge.
        - Discussing models, equations, and data as figures.
        - Providing your narrative argument or voice.
    - Past tense is used for:
        - Describing what was done, especially in experiments or methods (not protocols).
        - Referring to samples or people, though occasionally these may still be in the present if unequivocally true at the time of writing.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Pay attention to journal-specific rules regarding voice (active vs. passive).
- If describing a method or results of an experiment, use past tense. 
- For general facts, models, or permanent features of your results (e.g., figures, universally true findings), use present tense.
- Consistency within the section or paper is essential. Don’t mix tenses without reason.
- Clarify temporal context for the reader: if reporting data from a specific year, use past tense and cite accordingly.

## Unclear Areas

- No sections are completely illegible or unclear, though the transition from present to past tense in the context of samples and people is remarked upon as less clear, suggesting edge-case ambiguity rather than textual uncertainty.
