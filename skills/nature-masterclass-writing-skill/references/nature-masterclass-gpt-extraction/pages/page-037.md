## Writing Principles

- Clearly present editorial and peer review processes using labeled diagrams to communicate complex workflows.
- Use quantitative data (percentages, weekly publication numbers, acceptance rates) to provide context for selectivity and process stages.
- Acknowledge variability in processes between different journals, and even between journals within a single publisher or family.
- Summarize key information visually, but always support visuals with explanatory text.

## Style / Structure Notes

- The page combines a structured diagram with annotated statistics and a succinct paragraph explaining the process and its variability.

- Important details, such as acceptance rates and numbers of published papers, are highlighted in the diagram itself, making statistical information accessible at a glance.
- Terminology is precise ("referee," "editorial criteria," "initial screening") and the explanation is tailored for readers interested in the logistics of scientific journal publishing.
