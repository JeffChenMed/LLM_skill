**At the sentence level**

**Keep your sentences uncomplicated and clear,** using the rules above.

**Break up long sentences.** Remember that each sentence should contain a single idea. Often, the most engaging text contains a range of sentence lengths, with some short, impactful statements in short sentences, and some more complicated ideas presented in longer sentences. But very long sentences with multiple clauses should be avoided. If you find a sentence in an existing draft that leaves you out of breath, try to identify each separate idea, and break the sentence up into shorter sentences.

**Use sentence construction to make your point.** Key phrases should be at the beginning or at the end of the sentence, where they carry emphasis.

**Explicitly guide readers through your argument.** Use transitions to link ideas together. You should try to avoid changing topic within any section of the paper without an explicit link between the ideas. Useful transitions include: however, for example, specifically, moreover, therefore etc.).

Conversely, where you are still talking about the same idea, a small amount of repetition of key ideas can help you make this clear to the reader.

**At the paragraph level**

Paragraphs are the fundamental organisation unit of your paper – each one groups together a few sentences on one ‘bigger idea’, or theme. This is a pivotal way of creating a clear flow of your ideas, arguments and concepts throughout your paper.

Paragraphs are constructed in a similar way to sentences, but the start and the end carry even more emphasis. So make sure that your paragraphs are short and structured, they make distinct points at the beginning, middle and end, and they are linked together with transitions.

## Writing Principles

- Keep sentences simple and clear.
- Each sentence should express one main idea.
- Vary sentence lengths for engaging writing, but avoid very long, complicated sentences.
- Break up long sentences into shorter, more digestible statements.
- Place key phrases at the beginning or end of sentences for emphasis.
- Use transitions to guide readers through your argument and show relationships between ideas.
- Repeat key ideas slightly when needed to clarify continuity.
- Paragraphs should center on one main theme or idea, grouping related sentences.
- Paragraphs should have clear structure: distinct beginnings, middles, and ends.
- Use transitions to link paragraphs and maintain the flow of argument or discussion.
