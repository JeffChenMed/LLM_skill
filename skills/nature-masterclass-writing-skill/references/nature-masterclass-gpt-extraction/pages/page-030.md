## Writing Principles

- Focus on guiding readers to the key, most important features of your data.
- Avoid overwhelming readers with every detail; focus on clarity and significance.
- Explicitly state your main findings, supported by appropriate statistics or visuals.
- Provide some concise interpretation of what the results mean in the experimental context, but save broader discussion for later sections.
- Ensure all tables, figures, and panels are clearly numbered in sequence as cited in your text.
- Use supplementary information for secondary or peripheral results if allowed by your target journal.
- Adhere to principles of clarity and brevity.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Begin the results section by restating the study objective and summarizing the methods relevant to the results being presented.
- Separate the significance and interpretation of the results into a clear, connected statement immediately after presenting the data.
- Organize data description in a logical order, often following the sequence of figures or tables.
- Use comparative and causal language carefully: show how changing variables affect outcomes (“increase with,” “suggest that,” “shifts the bottleneck”).
- Explicitly describe the meaning of key data patterns or trends.
