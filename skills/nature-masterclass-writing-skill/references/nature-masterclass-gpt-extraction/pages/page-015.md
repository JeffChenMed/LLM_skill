## Transcription

So besides containing great data and conclusions, a great paper must tell a great story, with a compelling narrative that guides readers through the argument and encourages them to continue reading.

Here we highlight some of the most common mistakes in scientific writing – many of which apply just as much to those who are native-English speakers as to those who are not – and provide some simple rules to follow.

**The golden rule of scientific writing**

**Always write for your intended target audience – not for yourself or your immediate peers.**

This means putting yourself in other people’s shoes:

- Would someone in a similar field to your own (the similarity depending on the likely breadth of interest in your conclusions) understand your conclusions, why they are interesting, how they change our understanding and where they lead us next?
- Would a fellow specialist in your field (but without your exact background) be able to follow your argument in full, audit all of your claims and repeat your experiments?

This is partly a matter of including specific information in the paper – try not to implicitly assume that readers know something that they are unlikely to know. However, writing style can make a big difference.

## Writing Principles

- **Always write with your audience in mind:** Scientific writing should be aimed at your target readers, not yourself or colleagues who share your precise expertise.
- **Empathize with your reader:** Consider whether someone in a related but not identical field would grasp your conclusions, appreciate their significance, and recognize their implications.
- **Be clear for fellow specialists:** Ensure your logic, claims, and methods are sufficiently documented so that others in your broad field can verify your results or repeat your work.
- **Avoid assumptions:** Do not implicitly assume readers know specialized information—explicitly provide background and context as necessary.
- **Writing style matters:** Clarity, completeness, and consideration for the reader’s perspective improve understanding and impact.
