## Writing Principles

- When responding to reviewers, explicitly acknowledge the reviewer's concern, provide evidence or justification, and specify any changes made to the manuscript as a result.
- If a limitation in study scope is raised, quantify the study's relevance (e.g., using percentage coverage of global biomes) and clarify how the manuscript reflects this scope.
- Clearly explain methodological choices, especially when they diverge from standard statistical assumptions (e.g., handling of replicates and independence).
- When revising figures/tables/analyses due to reviewer feedback, clearly state the specific alterations and the rationale behind maintaining or changing particular methods.
- Provide comparative results (e.g., R² values, predictive power) to support the validity of alternative or revised statistical models.
- Use visual elements (figures, model outputs) to validate narrative claims and support conclusions.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Each reviewer comment is followed by a labeled and detailed "Response," indicating a point-by-point reply structure.
- Technical and procedural changes are specified by referencing exact lines, figures, and supplementary materials.
- Responses directly answer concerns—quantifying, justifying, or conceding as appropriate—while summarizing manuscript modifications.
- Statistical details (sample size, model performance metrics) are explicitly stated for transparency.
- Figure captions are concise but clarify the essence of the depicted data/model.
