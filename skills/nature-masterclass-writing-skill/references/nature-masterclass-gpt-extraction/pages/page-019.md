This example is chronologically ordered. Please note that references were removed from the sample text for clarity. Bottom-up paragraphs are especially common in Results sections like this, as they follow the logical order of scientific experiments. First the aim (not present here), then the method (often merged into a sentence with the findings) then the findings, and finally the conclusion in the very last sentence. 

Often the final sentence will lead directly into the next sentence, e.g. “Next, we determined [something building on that previous conclusion]”.

This is much more effective—and more sympathetic to readers—than mentioning the results even a few words before (or without) describing the experiment that generated those results. This does happen in some manuscripts.

## Writing Principles

- Organize information chronologically in Results sections for clarity—beginning with the aim, followed by the method, then findings, and concluding with results.
- Ensure each paragraph follows a logical flow, with one sentence building naturally onto the next.
- Always consider your target audience and aim to communicate for a broader scientific readership, not just close peers.
- Use short, straightforward sentences for clarity and ease of understanding.
- Structure paragraphs logically and ensure they link together for a clear narrative.
- Review your message for effectiveness and clarity, possibly seeking feedback from peers to improve language and communication.

## Style / Structure Notes

- “Bottom-up” paragraphing (starting with methods, then findings, then conclusions) aids reader comprehension in scientific writing, especially in the Results section.
- Direct connection between sentences helps narrative continuity; avoid presenting results before describing the corresponding methods.
- Bullet points are used to emphasize practical writing advice.
