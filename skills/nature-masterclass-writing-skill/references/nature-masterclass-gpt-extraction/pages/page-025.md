## Writing Principles

- Evaluate both the **clarity** and **targeting** of scientific titles and abstracts:  
  - Consider which groups (readers, disciplines, specialists, or generalists) the text is aiming to attract.
  - A successful title or abstract clearly sets expectations and conveys the core subject of the paper.
  - Omission of critical information in titles/abstracts may result in missed understanding by experts vetting relevant literature.

- Assess **consistency** between abstract and full paper:
  - Compare the impressions given by an abstract with the detailed content of the main text (introduction, figures, discussion).
  - Be critical of “hype” or omissions in abstracts that fail to mention key findings expanded upon in the full text.

## Style / Structure Notes

- Use **reflective questions** to examine scientific writing, e.g., asking what audiences are being addressed or what is omitted.
- Titles and abstracts should be **self-contained** and explicit enough for specialists scanning lists to grasp the central contribution or topic.
- Abstracts should accurately preview the entire paper and avoid exaggeration (“hype”) or significant omission of results.
