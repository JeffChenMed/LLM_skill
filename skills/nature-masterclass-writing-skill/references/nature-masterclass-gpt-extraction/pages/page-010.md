## Transcription

Here we provide some general guidelines to follow when considering how to display your data and images.

**Decide what your main message is.** Determine the best graph type for your data. It may seem obvious but choosing the correct plot type will display the data correctly, allowing readers to see the most important message immediately.

Bar charts are suitable for displaying data such as counts and lengths that start at zero. The bar provides a convenient proxy for the quantity of data and means it is easy for readers to compare multiple measurements.

A common error is using a bar chart to display data that would be better presented in a box plot. Box plots are typically used in biomedical research in which statisticians have historically had considerable input into study design and analysis. This is also because box plots are best suited for showing the distribution of data from large samples, for example samples from multiple patients, or population studies, which are typically found in the biomedical sciences.

Ideally, the outlier points should be plotted too, overlaid onto the box plot to give further transparency as to the distribution.

A good example of this is found in this paper:

Recurrent somatic mutations in regulatory regions of human cancer genomes,  
_Nature Genetics_ (2015)¹²

A _Nature Methods_ blog post on box plots from the 30th January 2014, called ‘Bring on the box plots’¹³ is an excellent starting point for finding out more.

## Writing Principles

- Identify your main message before deciding how to present your data.
- Select a graph type that best fits your data and message; this choice directly impacts clarity and immediacy of communication.
- Bar charts are best for simple counts or measurements that can be directly compared.
- Box plots are preferred for displaying distributions from larger samples or populations, particularly in biomedical contexts.
- Avoid misusing bar charts for data better represented with box plots.
- Always display outliers in box plots to give transparent, honest data representation.
- Refer to published literature and expert sources when determining best practices for data visualization.
