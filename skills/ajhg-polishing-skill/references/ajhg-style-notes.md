# AJHG Style Notes

Use these notes after reading `ajhg-dyadic-approach-style-exemplar.pdf`, or as a backup when direct PDF reading is unavailable.

## Exemplar

The bundled exemplar is an AJHG Perspective:

`A dyadic approach to the delineation of diagnostic entities in clinical genomics`

Its value for this skill is stylistic rather than topical. Do not import its disease taxonomy argument into unrelated manuscripts.

## Prose Pattern

- Open from a conceptual problem, not from broad field importance.
- State complexity calmly, then identify the need for a clearer framework.
- Move from established knowledge to the unresolved problem, then to the proposed contribution.
- Use the contribution to clarify, reconcile, formalize, designate, reflect, support, establish, or test.
- Let repeated terms remain stable when they carry the argument.

## Sentence Rhythm

- Favor short or moderate sentences with one principal proposition. Use a longer balanced sentence only when its main clause remains immediately clear.
- Use contrastive transitions sparingly and precisely: `whereas`, `while`, `yet`, `however`, `in contrast`, `concurrently`.
- Use scope qualifiers to avoid overclaiming: `preferably`, `whenever feasible`, `when justified`, `in some cases`, `in this setting`.
- Use appositive explanation for technical concepts, but keep the main clause readable.
- Prefer formal subject nouns over conversational framing: `This approach`, `These findings`, `The current framework`, `The primary analysis`.
- Avoid em dashes, en dashes used as prose punctuation, and suspended hyphens such as `gene- or disease-level`. Recast the syntax rather than deleting the mark mechanically.

## Tone

- Calm, declarative, and concept-first.
- Normative when justified, but not polemical.
- Elegant through precision rather than ornament.
- Clear about complexity without becoming vague.

## Avoid

- Promotional adjectives.
- Rhetorical questions.
- Casual emphasis.
- Synonym cycling for key terms.
- Borrowing the exemplar's specific examples, nomenclature proposal, or citations unless the user's manuscript is actually about that topic.
